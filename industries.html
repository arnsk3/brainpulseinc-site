The financial services context

Financial services has the longest history of model risk governance of any commercial sector. The Federal Reserve's SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12 supervisory guidance on model risk management has been operational since 2011 â predating the current wave of AI/ML by a decade. Banks have always had model validation functions, model inventories, and ongoing performance monitoring. What's changed is the surface area: AI/ML models for credit decisions, fraud detection, anti-money laundering, customer service, and risk pricing have expanded the scope dramatically while introducing failure modes that traditional model risk management was not designed to handle.

The regulatory environment has tightened in response. The CFPB has issued AI-specific guidance on adverse action notices, model explainability, and discriminatory outcomes. The ECOA (Equal Credit Opportunity Act) and Fair Housing Act apply to algorithmic decisions. The FTC, OCC, FDIC, and state attorneys general have all signaled increased enforcement. The Treasury Department has issued AI risk guidance through OFR and FSOC. Internationally, the EU AI Act classifies most consumer credit decisions as high-risk AI, triggering full Article 6 obligations for any financial services firm with EU exposure.
